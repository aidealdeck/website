Why most agents know their closings and not their numbers

You know how many deals you closed. Ask how many leads it took, or how many of your listing appointments turned into signed agreements, and most agents guess. The guess is usually too kind. A KPI tracker replaces the guess with a count, and the count tells you which step of your business leaks.

It does not need to be complicated. Six numbers, counted the same way every week, are enough to run a real estate business by the numbers.

The six numbers to count

  1. Leads. Every new person who raised a hand: a form, a call, a referral, a sign call, an open-house visitor who gave you a name. Count them all, and tag the source.
  2. Appointments set. A time on the calendar with a buyer or seller. Not "we should meet sometime".
  3. Appointments held. You actually met. The gap between set and held is your no-show rate.
  4. Signed. A buyer agency or listing agreement with a signature on it.
  5. Under contract. An accepted offer.
  6. Closed. Money in. This is the one you already know.

Write a one-line definition for each number before you start, and count by that line every week. The definitions matter more than the spreadsheet; a tracker with fuzzy definitions tells you fuzzy things.

The percentages between the steps are the point

The six counts give you five conversion rates: lead to appointment set, set to held, held to signed, signed to under contract, under contract to closed. Multiply them together and you get your real lead-to-closed rate.

Those five rates show where to work. If 150 leads become 58 appointments set and only 42 held, you do not have a lead problem, you have a confirmation problem. If 42 held become 21 signed, your appointment presentation needs attention before you spend another dollar on leads. The tracker makes that visible on one screen instead of in your memory.

Work backwards from your goal to one weekly number

This is the part most agents skip, and the part that changes behavior. Three divisions:

  1. Closings needed = your GCI goal ÷ your average GCI per closing. Example: $300,000 ÷ $12,000 = 25 closings.
  2. Leads needed = closings needed ÷ your lead-to-closed rate. Example: 25 ÷ 10% = 250 leads for the year.
  3. Your weekly number = leads needed ÷ weeks left. Example: 250 ÷ 50 = 5 new leads a week. Or, one step down the funnel, about 2 appointments set a week.

Now you have one number to hit each week, and a dashboard that tells you whether you hit it. When you are behind, you know it in week 3, not in November. The KPI Tracker does these divisions for you from the goal you type in, and recalculates as the year goes on. The live demo lets you move the goal and the average and watch the weekly number change.

Which lead sources actually close

Tag every lead with where it came from and the closed deals sort themselves by source. The surprise is almost always the same: the source that produces the most leads is rarely the source that produces the most closings, and the cost per closing differs by a factor of five or more between sources. Once you can see closed GCI by source next to what you spent on each, deciding where next quarter's money goes stops being a debate.

Keeping it true

A tracker only helps if the numbers go in. Ten minutes on Monday is enough: last week's leads with their sources, appointments set and held, anything signed, anything that went under contract or closed. Where Claude can read your CRM or your sheet, it can update the tracker for you on the schedule you set; where it cannot, a spreadsheet export from your CRM does the job.

Two honest notes. The tracker does not get you more leads or closings; it shows you your numbers and what needs your attention, and results depend on your work. And AI can make mistakes, so check what Claude sets up and check important numbers before you rely on them.
