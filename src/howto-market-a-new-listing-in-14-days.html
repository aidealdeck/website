Why a fixed two-week plan beats a good memory

The first two weeks decide most listings. That is when the most buyers see it, when the feedback is freshest and when a price conversation is still easy. Most real estate pros know this and still launch each listing a little differently, because the steps live in their head and their head is busy. A written plan with a task for each day fixes that, and it gives you something a busy seller rarely gets: a report that shows the work.

Below is the shape of a launch that works for most residential listings. The Listing Exposure Playbook gives you the day-by-day version with fill-in templates, a checklist and the two seller reports.

Days 1 to 3: prepare, so go-live is one day, not a week

  • Photos and, if the price point supports it, video or a floor plan. Nothing goes live with phone photos.
  • Measurements, disclosures, the listing copy and the room-by-room feature list written and checked.
  • The launch calendar agreed with the seller: the go-live date, the open-house date, and the two report dates. Put the report dates in writing; they are the promise you will keep.
  • The sign ordered, the lockbox ready, showing instructions written.

Day 4: go live everywhere on the same day

MLS live in the morning. Check the syndication to the big portals the same day, because a listing with a missing photo or a wrong bedroom count on a portal costs you the first weekend. Sign in the yard. Announcement to your database, your past clients and your social channels, each written for that audience rather than one message pasted everywhere. Your MLS and your brokerage have rules about advertising and about what you may say; the plan runs inside them.

Days 5 to 7: the first weekend and the day-7 report

  • Showings and the open house. Every showing agent gets a feedback request the same day, and a second one if they do not answer.
  • Track three numbers daily: showings, online views and saves where the portals show them, and feedback received.
  • On day 7 the seller gets the first report: what was done, how many showings and views, what buyers and agents said, and what you recommend for week two. Sellers who get a written report on day 7 ask fewer anxious questions on day 9.

Days 8 to 13: the second push

Fresh content keeps a listing from going stale: a new lead photo, a short walk-through, a post about the neighborhood. Follow up with every agent who showed it. If the week-one feedback points at price or presentation, this is the week to have that conversation, with the showing count and the feedback in front of the seller instead of your opinion alone.

Day 14: report and decide

The day-14 report covers both weeks: total showings, online activity, every piece of feedback, what changed after the day-7 report, and a clear recommendation: hold, adjust the price, adjust the presentation, or both. Two written reports in two weeks is more than most sellers have ever received, and it is what makes the next price conversation a business decision rather than an argument.
